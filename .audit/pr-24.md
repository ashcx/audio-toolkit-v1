## Why
Sprint 24 makes normalization target measured loudness or true peak, and adds an optional compressor. Speech levels become consistent without silently clipping.

## What changed
- Normalize has Loudness and Peak modes. Loudness uses gated integrated LUFS and a true-peak ceiling. New Peak steps target dBTP; legacy peak recipes keep their sample-peak meaning.
- The PCM worker measures K-weighted loudness and true peak, applies the ceiling after gain, and reports achieved and limited results.
- An optional compressor with linked detection, speech/music presets, amount, preview gain reduction and no makeup gain.
- Results are qualified at the Normalize step when later gain, compressor, channels or isolation steps can change the final level.

## Scope
Sprint 24 only. No waveform trimming, stem selection or encoding modes. Listening and physical-device acceptance stay with the user. Lossy decoded exports are still not metered.

## Tradeoffs
- True-peak meters can disagree near Nyquist: a 16 kHz burst had the app at -1.3 dBTP while ffmpeg measured the export at -0.4 dBTP, 0.1 dB inside a -0.3 dBTP ceiling, and a phase sweep read up to 0.64 dB above ffmpeg. The loudness ceiling therefore keeps a 1.0 dB guard. Peak mode does not: it aims at the requested dBTP directly, so the target is reachable. Reported peaks remain meter estimates.
- Legacy peak recipes keep a dBFS sample-peak editor. New peak steps use dBTP.
- Achieved loudness is calculated from the single measured pass; later steps are not re-metered, so the result is qualified instead of guessing.

## Blast Radius
Touches the recipe schema, planner, PCM worker, normalize/compressor controls and result rows. Legacy peak recipes retain their meaning. The compressor is off by default.
